## Concrete failures and boundary rules

- An initial opposing defender can appear on the actor's current roster. The old initial map could still accept an opposing block. Such a conflict now excludes the outcome, keeps its candidate in unresolved coverage and contributes no favorable score. An omitted later roster cannot resurrect the conflicting initial claim.
- A known purchase/removal invalidates only its numeric MercenaryId. A confirmed selected purchase binds to its independently checked first following roster. Without that selected identity, a positive subsequent roster can establish ownership separately; an earlier/future roster cannot. Known inner Data.Id/TeamId contradictions and duplicate/unknown roster team namespaces are conflicts. Two rosters with both team IDs omitted cannot establish a new owner.
- Initial overlapping IDs remain unresolved unless a qualified new identity resolves the overlap. Names do not supply the current identity; historical names are withheld after a replacement source.
- The buyer's EventBuyMercenary/EventRemoveMercenary.GamerId is an observed identifier, not a team or choice-controller slot. Public demo7's opaque buyer is classified without exporting its contents. A numeric-buyer0/current-owner1 counterexample prevents reintroducing a cross-namespace assumption. Recruitment catalogue data is not a current full trait/eligibility list.
- Block attacker, defender and chooser have distinct roles. Only CanChoseDie1 identifies the actual chooser slot; a recipient-only question does not confer control. The source-specific chooser must agree with the participant designated by AttackerChoice. Team-reroll offer, decision and result also need matching time-local ownership and a compatible verified slot. Loner/Pro/own-skill and treatment/embedded-damage lookups use the same time-local index.
- An unrecognized transition interrupts ownership evidence, including for an initial player. A later positive roster renews ownership; an omission cannot. Known ordinary action, choice, damage, removal-from-pitch and effect records preserve roster-instance IDs separately from their physical/skill consequences. Recognized typed PlayerStep/AccuracyStep layouts, ThrowTeamMateStep13, selected effect layouts and treatment/resource receipt layouts have owner-only invariants. This does not grant an unverified action label, probability policy, skill applicability, choice legality or tactical availability. Unknown typed step codes/extra step fields, unrecognized effect layouts and malformed identity mutations do not preserve this evidence.

Existing `traitTransitions.ts` preserves the prior, narrower intrinsic-trait rules. Ownership-only continuity does not extend those rules. Effects, transformations, missing lists and purchases/removals still invalidate or withhold current trait applicability as required by the separate trait reducer. The tactical reducers consume the original parser view and retain their own ownership/clock/availability/continuity guards. Applying the new probability-role annotations must not manufacture a context point or resource claim.
